# Getting the Remote Job (Phase 7)

The dirty secret: most good quant roles are never publicly posted. They fill
through networks, referrals, and specialist recruiters.

## Where quant jobs actually live
- **LinkedIn** — filter "quantitative analyst" + "remote" + "entry level"; set job
  alerts; apply the same day a role posts — good roles close fast.
- **Glassdoor** — check company reviews; culture varies from collegial (Jane
  Street's reputation) to brutal.
- **QuantConnect job board** — direct line to firms hiring algorithmic traders.
- **eFinancialCareers** — specialist finance board; better signal-to-noise than
  Indeed for quant.
- **Seeking Alpha forums** — practitioners talk; can lead to informal referrals.
- **Numerai** — top leaderboard performers get recruited directly.

## Remote reality check
Full-remote at hedge funds is **rare**, especially early-career, because the work
involves collaboration and mentorship that's hard to replicate on Slack. But
remote exists in these niches:
- **Crypto quant funds** — DeFi-native firms are often fully remote, globally
  distributed.
- **Fintech companies** — risk modeling, fraud detection, pricing models. Quant
  skills transfer completely.
- **AI + finance startups** — infrastructure layer; often remote-first.
- **Freelance / consulting** — Toptal, Upwork; backtesting, research, model-building
  contracts.

## Salary ranges
- Entry-level base (US): **$82K–$150K**.
- Hedge-fund entry QR total comp: **$200K–$300K**.
- Remote crypto quant: **$60K–$250K**.

## Interviews — what they actually ask
- **Quant Developer**: LeetCode Medium coding, system-design basics, moderate
  probability. Practice on HackerRank/LeetCode; focus on dynamic programming and
  graph algorithms.
- **Quant Researcher**: probability brainteasers ("you roll two dice…"), mental-math
  speed (practice on **Zetamac** — a score above 50 is competitive), statistics at
  depth. They test how you reason, not what you memorized.
- **Quant Trader**: fast probability reasoning, market-making sims ("I'll buy at 3,
  sell at 5 — what's your edge?"). Decision speed under pressure over math depth.

**Universal advice:** when stuck, say what you're thinking — "I think the approach
is X, but let me check the edge case." Interviewers grade your reasoning process.
Silence is worse than a partially-worked answer.

## The realistic timeline
- **M1–2:** Python fundamentals + finance vocabulary; understand a Sharpe ratio.
- **M3–4:** First backtest running; SMA crossover in a simulator; 1 GitHub project.
- **M5–6:** Pairs trading + factor model added; learning ML for finance; applying to
  entry-level QD roles at smaller firms.
- **M7–9:** 3–5 GitHub projects; Numerai account; CQF/EPAT if budget allows;
  actively interviewing.
- **M10–12:** First offers at mid-tier firms, prop shops, or fintech — **$70K–$150K**.
- **Year 2–3:** With a track record and network, move toward larger funds or more
  senior roles. QR becomes realistic without a PhD if the portfolio is strong enough.

## The one thing that separates people who make it
**Honest analysis.** "This strategy has a 0.4 Sharpe, 22% max drawdown, and falls
apart in volatile regimes — here's why" beats a 40% backtest with no risk metrics.
The discipline to test rigorously, report the failures, and learn from them is what
separates practitioners from the hopeful.
