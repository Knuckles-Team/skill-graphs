# The Quant Roles

"Quant" is a family of jobs at the intersection of math, code, and finance. The
skill sets differ — know which one you're training for.

## Quantitative Researcher (QR)
The idea person. Finds alpha signals in data, builds predictive models, designs
strategies. Most academically demanding role. Top-tier firms (Jane Street,
Citadel, Two Sigma) typically expect a PhD.

## Quantitative Developer (QD)
The builder. Takes a researcher's model and turns it into production-grade code.
Python, C++, systems design. **The most accessible entry point without a PhD** —
ideal if you come from a CS background.

## Quantitative Trader (QT)
Executes strategies in real time. Part math, part intuition. Interviews are
famously fast: probability puzzles, mental math, dice games. Market-making shops
recruit heavily here.

## Risk Analyst
Uses quant methods to measure how much the fund can lose. Slower-paced than
trading. Base pay **$80K–$250K**.

## Realistic first target
For most beginners: **Quant Developer or Risk Analyst at a mid-tier firm**, with
**Quant Researcher** as the long-term goal.

## Compensation snapshot
- Entry-level base (US firms): **$82K–$150K**.
- Hedge-fund entry-level QR total comp (incl. bonus): **$200K–$300K**.
- Remote crypto quant: **$60K–$250K**, highly dependent on fund and performance share.

## The mindset that gets people hired
Demonstrated ability over diplomas. Recruiters at mid-tier funds and prop shops
hire candidates with zero finance experience when their GitHub shows **rigorous
thinking and honest analysis**.
